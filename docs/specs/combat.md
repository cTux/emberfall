# Combat, enemies and rewards

Status: implemented baseline. Goal: Responsive class combat with authoritative damage, cooperative scaling and clear threats. See [technical design](../design/simulation.md).

## COMBAT-01 — Attacks and ailments

Warrior keeps its 5-damage slash. Ranger and Mage each launch two base-spell projectiles; Druid launches one projectile aimed around the cursor or nearest auto-target, with 6 degrees between adjacent projectiles. The shared spread formula centers odd counts on the aim (three: -6, 0, +6 degrees) and even counts around it (two: -3, +3; four: -9, -3, +3, +9 degrees). Projectiles maintain their launch direction until impact; Druid bounces track each selected target. Ranger arrows deal 5 damage and pierce with a 1000-unit travel limit. Mage fireballs travel up to 250 units with auto-target enabled; each impact deals 3 direct damage and explodes in a 100-unit radius for 1 damage to other enemies. The direct target receives no explosion damage from that projectile. All four classes have a one-second base attack cooldown. Bleed, poison, and burn retain their 10% proc chance, uncapped stacks, 1 damage per stack per second, and shared five-second expiry refreshed by new stacks. Damage over time does not trigger further ailments; the latest applying player receives kill credit for that ailment.

Druid launches one root projectile with a 250-unit travel limit when auto-target is enabled. It hits its main target, then bounces up to three times to the nearest living enemy within 250 units of each impact, using wrapped distance and excluding every previously hit target. The same projectile stays visible between impacts, with artwork at half its previous size and an unchanged 4-unit collision radius. Each bounce tracks its selected target, skips other enemies and ends if that target dies or no eligible next target remains. After the fourth hit it ends. All hits deal full weapon power (3 damage with the starter staff before critical hits). Surviving ordinary enemies receive one root stack that slows movement by 10% for 5 seconds. Further hits refresh expiry to 5 seconds after the latest hit without adding stacks or duration. Roots deal no damage over time and do not affect bosses; bosses still take impact damage. Root kills use the shared XP, loot, and boss-completion paths.

Sword aim tracks the closest living enemy every rendered frame and server tick, rotating along the shortest arc with shared 80 ms exponential smoothing (about 240 ms to complete 95% of a turn). Both hit detection and the green zone use this smoothed angle. During each 260 ms active swing (1000 ms base cooldown), the server checks every enemy collision body against the green half-disc, including radius and diameter edges and forest wrapping. An enemy can take 5 damage only once per swing, even if it enters late or aim changes. The green pulse and sword animation use the server swing phase rather than an independent attack timer.

## COMBAT-02 — Encounters and scaling

Enemies target the nearest living forest player using wrapped distances. Shared deterministic steering routes around nearby trunks, and a wrapped spatial grid separates bodies according to their radii (8–16 units). Every tenth successful spawn is an elite (50 HP, yellow health bar); normal enemies have 10 HP before archetype and party modifiers. Each additional player in the forest multiplies enemy HP by 1.75 and dropped XP by 1.2: with N scene members, the multipliers are `1.75 ** max(0, N - 1)` and `1.2 ** max(0, N - 1)`. Three players give 3.0625 times HP and 1.44 times dropped XP. Village players and companions do not count; dead scene members do. Existing enemies and pending spawns adjust on membership changes while retaining their health percentage; new spawns, elites and bosses use the same HP multiplier. XP drop amounts are fixed at death, shared with every living scene player on collection, and saved without rounding; the existing 1 XP kill credit is unchanged. Skeletons move at 64 units/second, small slime runners at 104, large bear brutes at 40, and ranged eye casters at 54. All hits deal 10 damage with a one-second per-player damage cooldown. Melee windups are 500–1000 ms: a ground circle fills red before damage, and targets outside its range are not hit. Missed attacks resume chasing. Casters stop within 210 units, wind up for 900 ms and fire red projectiles at 210 units/second toward the marked location. Projectiles use swept substeps, wrap, collide with trees and players, and expire after 2.5 seconds. All enemy spawns, including bosses, have a one-second red zone before becoming solid; occupied spawns wait until their space clears. The server owns attacks, damage, deaths, votes, membership and deadlines. Enemy spawning is bounded at 160 per scene; network snapshots are still full snapshots, not a large-horde scalability claim.

Non-boss spawns roll once: 10% brute (3x HP), 10% caster (0.7x HP), otherwise regular skeleton/fast melee. Modifiers multiply normal/elite HP; boss base HP remains 200 before party scaling. Debuff icons and stack counts appear above enemy health bars. All classes use distinct supplied character sprites, walking and attack animations. No talent gameplay is introduced yet.

## COMBAT-03 — Bear

Each Druid has a permanent companion named Bear in village and forest, with its own health bar and 1.5x the player's maximum HP. Bear targets and chases the nearest living enemy within 200 units of its owner, using the warrior's 88-unit forward slash geometry, 260 ms active window and 1000 ms base cadence, but claws deal 2 damage once per enemy per swing. Bear moves at 1.3x player speed (234 units/second) for chasing, returning, and dodging in both areas. Bear approaches an 80-unit gap instead of the enemy center, retreats to restore that spacing when enemies close in, and prioritizes escaping telegraphed attack circles and sidestepping projectiles predicted to pass within 30 units in the next 0.35 seconds. Bear chooses its target and movement destination once per second, then moves smoothly toward that point; short warnings between decisions, hazards, and blocked escape routes can still damage Bear. Movement retains tree avoidance; village buildings, wardrobe and torch posts do not block the companion. A companion created, resurrected or teleported inside a trunk can move out without entering trees from outside. Its walking animation plays only while it moves. Beyond 200 units of wrapped distance from its owner it stops attacking and returns; within 20 units it resumes hunting on its next decision. Beyond 500 pixels it immediately teleports to its owner, using wrapped distance in both areas. Enemies can target and damage Bear with melee and projectiles; death schedules full-health resurrection at the owner exactly five seconds later. A dead owner suspends hunting. Companion state is temporary, server-owned Player state, interpolated in snapshots and repositioned on area transfers; character saves continue to persist class progress, with fresh Druid progress added automatically to older saves.

In the new runtime, Warrior has a 75-unit base slash range. Bear inherits the player's equipment stats, including power, attack speed, critical chance and multiplier, and armor, with physical damage, a fixed 75-pixel claw range and 1.5× the player's maximum HP. Bear moves at player speed. Each claw hit has a 10% chance to inflict the same stacking five-second bleed as Warrior; damage and bleed credit the owner. An unarmed owner gives Bear no weapon attack. The 200-unit owner hunt/return leash, 80-unit spacing, decision timing and resurrection rules remain the same.

The original runtime retains the fixed claw damage, range and speed above.

## COMBAT-04 — Training

The lobby has two training clearings: one skeleton dummy on the west side and six in a 1/2/3 triangle on the east side. Each has 1,000,000,000 HP, replenished to full every second by the server. Dummies never move or attack and award no XP or loot. Player attacks, ailments, projectiles, and the companion use the same combat handling as the forest. In the new runtime, each area has a 270-unit horizontal radius and a 229.5-unit vertical radius, with no ground fill or circle outline. The original runtime retains its 135-unit horizontal and 114.75-unit vertical radii and ground indicators. Lobby auto-attacks and companion hunting start only while the player's feet are inside either area and stop on leaving; already-fired projectiles and ailments can finish. Forest combat is unchanged. Training state is shared within each world and separate from the forest scene.

## COMBAT-05 — Rewards and damage accounting

Combat presentation includes sword sparks, enemy attack sparks and short red blood bursts for damaged players and enemies. Killed enemies drop a glowing, gently hopping experience orb and independently have a 10% chance to drop a gold coin. Server-owned drops are collected within 22 units after a 300 ms initial hop; collection removes them for everyone and each experience orb grants its death-time amount (1 XP solo, multiplied by the party XP factor) to every living forest player, regardless of distance. Dead players and players in the lobby receive no pickup XP; new-runtime gold grants one saved coin to its collector (original-runtime gold remains visual only; existing kill XP is unchanged). Drops remain collectible after boss defeat, expire after 60 seconds, and are capped at 512 per scene. Glow textures are cached, offscreen drops are culled, and blood rendering is limited to 80 recent hit events.

Experience shards and coins attract after the initial hop toward the nearest living forest player within 140 units, moving at 280 units/second. XP pickup rewards all living scene players. Separately, the credited killer receives 1 XP at death; Bear damage credits its owner. Training grants neither reward. In the new runtime, gold grants one coin to its collector, saved per class; original-runtime gold remains visual only. Neither runtime has a spending system.

DPS is actual enemy damage over the previous five seconds divided by five, including ailments and Bear and excluding overkill. It returns to zero after five seconds without damage.

In the new runtime, players, enemies, training dummies and Bear have one circular damage hitbox centered on the whole visible model. Its diameter matches the rendered model size (48 units for players and Bear; 32–68 for enemy archetypes). Projectiles and melee overlap these bodies using wrapped geometry in both forest and training. Debug mode draws the same damage bodies without feet or projectile terrain overlays. Movement separation and terrain avoidance retain their existing rules; player movement still passes through scenery. Decorative scenery remains non-damageable.

## New-runtime equipment

In the `*-new` runtime, [CHAR-04](characters.md#char-04--equipment-new-runtime)
defines gear-derived power, attack speed, range, armor and typed critical damage.
The values above describe starter weapons before critical hits. The same shared
hit path applies in training and forest; existing ailment and companion rules
remain separate from weapon critical rolls.

## Acceptance

- A Warrior swing hits each overlapping enemy at most once during its active window, including late entrants and bodies across a seam.
- Ranger and Mage fire two straight projectiles with centered spread; Druid fires one. Fireball splash excludes its direct target; the root projectile stays visible through up to three bounces within 250 units of each impact, never repeating a target, for full damage, refreshing a single five-second 10% slow only on ordinary enemies.
- Ailments stack and refresh expiry without recursively applying ailments. Latest applicator receives ailment kill credit.
- Three forest members produce 3.0625× enemy HP and 1.44× dropped XP; village members and Bear do not count, dead scene members do. Membership changes preserve enemy health percentage.
- Telegraphs precede spawns and attacks; moving outside melee range avoids a hit. Boss death alone completes the encounter.
- Bear obeys chase/return/teleport boundaries, takes damage and resurrects after five seconds; a dead owner suspends hunting.
- Training uses the same attack paths, starts new attacks only inside its zones, and grants no XP or loot.
- Pickup removes a drop for everyone; dead/village players gain no pickup XP and fractional XP survives saving.
